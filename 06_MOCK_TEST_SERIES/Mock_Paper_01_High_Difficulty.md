## Domain 1: Classical Indian Philosophy (Q.1 – Q.25)

#### Q.1 Solution
- **Correct Option**: **(B)**
- **Primary Textual Reference**: Gangesa Upadhyaya, *Tattvacintamani* (Anumanakhanda, *Siddhanta-laksana* section); Mathuranatha Tarkavagisa, *Rahasya* commentary.
- **Philosophical Grounding**: In the *Tattvacintamani*, Gangesa examines five preliminary definitions of invariable concomitance (*vyaptipancaka*), such as *sadhyabhavavad-avrttitvam* (the non-existence of the probans in the locus of the absence of the probandum). These initial definitions fail in the case of universal affirmative propositions (*kevalanvayi anumana*), e.g., "The pot is nameable because it is knowable" (*ghato'bhidheyah prameyatvat*), because knowability and nameability exist in all things; consequently, there is no locus where the absence of nameability exists (*sadhyabhava* is unexampled or *aprasiddha*). To solve this, Gangesa formulates the *siddhanta-laksana*: *Vyapti* is the co-existence of the probans with a probandum which is not the counter-positive (*apratiyogi*) of an absence (*viraha/abhava*) that resides in the locus of the probans (*hetusamānādhikaraṇa*), where the absence is conditioned by the delimiter of the relation of the probandumness.
- **Distractor Diagnosis**:
  - *(A) is erroneous*: This is the first definition of the *vyaptipancaka*, which suffers from *avyapti* (under-applicability) in all *kevalanvayi* inferences where the absence of the probandum does not exist anywhere.
  - *(C) is erroneous*: *Anupadhika-sambandhah* (unconditional relation) is the ancient/prācīna Nyaya definition formulated by Udayana and Vacaspati Misra; it is discarded by Navya-Nyaya as circular, because defining an *upadhi* itself requires a prior definition of *vyapti*.
  - *(D) is erroneous*: This formulation represents an awkward double-negation variant which fails to incorporate the essential *avacchedaka* (limitor) restrictions needed to prevent over-extension (*ativyapti*) in cases of spatial and temporal partial co-existence (*samanyabhava* vs *visesabhava*).

#### Q.2 Solution
- **Correct Option**: **(A)**
- **Primary Textual Reference**: Jayanta Bhatta, *Nyayamanjari*; Visvanatha Nyayapancanana, *Bhasapariccheda* with *Siddhantamuktavali*, verses 63–65.
- **Philosophical Grounding**: Nyaya recognizes three kinds of extraordinary perceptual contact (*alaukika-sannikarsa*): (1) *Samanyalaksana* (perception of an entire class through the generic property), (2) *Jnanalaksana* (perception of an object through the medium of a past cognition/memory), and (3) *Yogaja* (intuitive perception born of yogic meditation). When one sees a piece of fragrant sandalwood from afar, the eyes cannot physically contact fragrance (which is an olfactory quality, *gandha*). However, upon seeing the sandalwood, the visual organ contacts the sandalwood, and the past reproductive memory-cognition (*purvajnana*) of fragrance serves as the sensory connection (*alaukika-pratyasatti*), producing the immediate perceptual judgment "The sandalwood is fragrant". This is classified strictly as visual perception (*cakshusha pratyaksa*) qualified by fragrance, not an inference, because it is accompanied by the phenomenal immediacy (*aparoksa-avabhasa*) characteristic of direct perception. Thus, both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) correctly explains (A).
- **Distractor Diagnosis**:
  - *(B) is erroneous*: (R) is precisely the causal and metaphysical mechanism formulated by the Naiyayikas to explain why the cognition is a perception (*pratyaksa*) rather than an inference (*anumiti*).
  - *(C) and (D) are erroneous*: Neither statement is false; both (A) and (R) are standard tenets of classical Nyaya-Vaisesika epistemology.

#### Q.3 Solution
- **Correct Option**: **(A)**
- **Primary Textual Reference**: Gautama, *Nyayasutra* I.2.4–9; Annambhatta, *Tarkasamgraha* and *Dipika*, sections on *Hetvabhasa*.
- **Philosophical Grounding**:
  - *Savyabhicara (Sadharana)* occurs when the probans exists in the locus of the probandum (*sapaksa*) as well as in the locus of its absence (*vipaksa*), e.g., "The mountain has fire because it is knowable" (knowability exists in burning hearths and in cold water bodies). (Matches 2).
  - *Satpratipaksa* (counter-balanced) occurs when the probans is opposed by an equally strong rival probans that establishes the non-existence of the probandum, e.g., "Sound is eternal because it is audible" countered by "Sound is non-eternal because it is a product". (Matches 3).
  - *Badhita* (sublated) occurs when the absence of the probandum in the subject (*paksa*) is ascertained beyond doubt by a stronger, prior *pramana* such as direct perception, e.g., "Fire is cold because it is a substance" (sublated directly by tactile perception). (Matches 1).
  - *Asiddha (Asrayasiddha)* occurs when the locus (*paksa*) itself is completely unreal or uninstantiated, e.g., "The sky-lotus is fragrant because it is a lotus" (there is no sky-lotus to bear fragrance). (Matches 4).

- **Distractor Diagnosis**:

#### Q.5 Solution
- **Correct Option**: **(C)**
- **Primary Textual Reference**: Salikanatha Misra, *Prakaranapancika* (*Nayavithi*); Prabhakara, *Brhati*.
- **Philosophical Grounding**: Prabhakara upholds *Akhyativada* (the non-apprehension theory of error). For Prabhakara, error is not a positive misapprehension, because all cognitions are intrinsically valid. When a shell is mistaken for silver, there are two distinct cognitions: (1) a direct sensory perception of a glittering 'this' (*idam*), and (2) a memory recollection of 'silver' (*rajata-smrti*) triggered by similarity. Because of a psychological defect, the memory-element (*smrtitvapramosa*) is forgotten, and the subject fails to apprehend the difference (*bhedagraha* or *asamsarga-agraha*) between the perceived 'this' and the remembered 'silver'. This non-discrimination prompts the person to reach forward. Thus, error is negative—a privation or failure of discrimination (*akhyati*).
- **Distractor Diagnosis**:
  - *(A) is erroneous*: This is *Anirvacaniyakhyati*, held exclusively by Advaita Vedanta.
  - *(B) is erroneous*: This is *Viparitakhyati*, upheld by the Bhatta Mimamsakas.
  - *(D) is erroneous*: This is *Anyathakhyati*, upheld by the Nyaya school, where real silver existing elsewhere is perceived here via *alaukika jnanalaksana-sannikarsa*.

#### Q.6 Solution
- **Correct Option**: **(A)**
- **Primary Textual Reference**: Prasastapada, *Padarthadharmasamgraha*; Sridhara, *Nyayakandali*.
- **Philosophical Grounding**: An opponent (particularly the Buddhist or Advaitin like Sriharsa) objects: If substance and quality are distinct, they need inherence (*samavaya*) to connect them; but then *samavaya* being distinct from the relata must need a second inherence to connect it to substance, and a third, generating an infinite regress (*anavastha*). Just as a conjunction (*samyoga*) is a quality requiring inherence, inherence is not a quality; it is an independent category that connects relata directly without needing an intermediate link. Hence, both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) explains why the regress is terminated.
