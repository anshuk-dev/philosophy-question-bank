### Question 1 (Format A: Conceptual MCQ - Śaṅkara's Definition of Adhyāsa)

In the opening preamble to his commentary on the *Brahmasūtra* (the celebrated *Adhyāsabhāṣya*), Śaṅkarācārya addresses the primary epistemological-metaphysical problem: How can superimposition (*adhyāsa*) occur between the Subject (*viṣayin*, pure consciousness / the 'I') and the Object (*viṣaya*, the insentient realm / the 'non-I'), given that they are as diametrically opposed as light and darkness (*tamaḥ-prakāśavad viruddha-svabhāva*)?

What is Śaṅkara’s exact technical definition of *Adhyāsa*, and how does he account for its possibility when the true Self (*Ātman*) is non-sensory and not an object (*aviṣaya*) presented before the eyes?

A) *Adhyāsa* is defined as *smṛtirūpaḥ paratra pūrvadṛṣṭāvabhāsaḥ* ("the appearance, in the form of memory, of something previously experienced in another locus"); it is possible because the *Ātman* is not entirely a non-object (*atyantam aviṣaya*), since it shines self-luminously as the object/referent of the 'I'-cognition (*asmat-pratyaya-viṣayatvāt*) and is immediately self-evident (*aparokṣatvāc ca*).  
B) *Adhyāsa* is defined as the physical transformation of the brain cells under the influence of Rajas and Tamas, proved by dreamless sleep.  
C) *Adhyāsa* is defined as *yathārtha-jñānam*, meaning all superimposition is actually true because God creates illusory objects to test human faith.  
D) *Adhyāsa* is the psychological projection of subconscious desires (*vāsanā*) onto the external world, possible because the soul has an atomic size and travels through the optic nerve.

---

#### Correct Answer:
**A) Adhyāsa is defined as smṛtirūpaḥ paratra pūrvadṛṣṭāvabhāsaḥ ("the appearance, in the form of memory, of something previously experienced in another locus"); it is possible because the Ātman is not entirely a non-object (atyantam aviṣaya), since it shines self-luminously as the object/referent of the 'I'-cognition (asmat-pratyaya-viṣayatvāt) and is immediately self-evident (aparokṣatvāc ca).**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Primary Source:
In Śaṅkarācārya’s *Adhyāsabhāṣya* (preamble to *Brahmasūtrabhāṣya*):
- Śaṅkara defines superimposition with technical perfection:
  $$\text{"āha—ko 'yam adhyāso nāmeti? ucyate—smṛti-rūpaḥ paratra pūrva-dṛṣṭāvabhāsaḥ."}$$
  ("It is asked: What is this superimposition? It is replied: It is the apparent presentation, in the mode of memory, of an object previously perceived in another locus.")
- The Purvapakṣa raises a serious objection: Superimposition normally occurs when an object present before the senses (like a piece of nacre) is mistaken for another previously perceived object (silver). But the inner Self (*Pratyagātman*) is partless, non-physical, and ungraspable by the senses (*aviṣaya*). How can the non-self (body, senses, mind) be superimposed upon the Self, or the Self upon the non-self?
- Śaṅkara resolves this objection definitively:
  $$\text{"na tāvad ayam ekāntenāviṣayaḥ, asmat-pratyaya-viṣayatvād aparokṣatvāc ca pratyag-ātma-prasiddheḥ."}$$
  ("The Self is not entirely a non-object; for it is known to exist as the object/referent of the 'I'-notion [*asmat-pratyaya*], and because the inner Self is immediately self-evident [*aparokṣa*] to everyone.")
  Nobody ever doubts "Do I exist or do I not exist?" Consciousness is immediately revealed (*svaprakāśa*). Hence, it possesses sufficient cognitive presence to serve as the substrate (*adhiṣṭhāna*) upon which the psycho-physical apparatus (the 'mine' and the 'I-maker') is mistakenly superimposed through beginningless ignorance (*anādir ananto naisargiko 'dhyāsaḥ*).

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **Option B is incorrect**: Advaita does not reduce *adhyāsa* to neuro-physiological or purely bodily transformations; *adhyāsa* is a transcendental, epistemological-metaphysical confusion of the Real and the Unreal (*satya-anṛte mithunī-kṛtya*).
- **Option C is incorrect**: This is the *Satkhyāti* position of Rāmānuja, which completely rejects Śaṅkara’s doctrine of *adhyāsa* as *mithyā*.
- **Option D is incorrect**: In Advaita, the *Ātman* is all-pervading (*vibhu*), never atomic (*aṇu*), and *adhyāsa* is not an optic journey through cranial nerves.

### Question 2 (Format C: Multi-Statement MSQ - The Post-Śaṅkara Schism: Bhāmatī vs. Vivaraṇa)

Following Śaṅkarācārya, classical Advaita Vedānta split into two prominent scholastic traditions: the **Bhāmatī school** (founded by Vācaspati Miśra) and the **Vivaraṇa school** (founded by Padmapāda and formalized by Prakāśātman).

Evaluate the following statements regarding the doctrinal divergences between these two schools:

I. Regarding the locus (*āśraya*) of cosmic ignorance (*avidyā*), the Bhāmatī school holds that the individual self (*jīva*) is the locus while Brahman is the content/object (*viṣaya*), whereas the Vivaraṇa school insists that Brahman alone is both the locus (*āśraya*) and the object (*viṣaya*) of *avidyā*.  
II. Regarding the ontological relation between *Jīva* and *Brahman*, the Bhāmatī school upholds the Limitation Theory (**Avacchedavāda**), whereas the Vivaraṇa school upholds the Reflection Theory (**Pratibimbavāda**).  
III. Regarding the instrumental cause (*karaṇa*) of liberating direct realization (*aparokṣa-jñāna*), the Bhāmatī school maintains that the mind (*manas*), purified by continuous meditation (*nididhyāsana*), is the direct instrument, whereas the Vivaraṇa school maintains that the scriptural Great Sentences (*Mahāvākyas*) themselves generate immediate perceptual realization (*śabda-aparokṣavāda*).  
IV. The Bhāmatī school posits a single, cosmic *avidyā* (*ekāvidyāvāda*), whereas the Vivaraṇa school posits an infinite multiplicity of distinct *avidyās* (*nānāvidyāvāda*) to explain why one jīva can be liberated without liberating all others.

Which of the statements given above are strictly **CORRECT**?

A) I, II, and III only  
B) I and IV only  
C) II, III, and IV only  
D) I, II, III, and IV

---

#### Correct Answer:
**A) I, II, and III only**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Primary Source:
In Vācaspati Miśra’s *Bhāmatī* and Prakāśātman’s *Pañcapādikā-Vivarana* (as well as Appayya Dīkṣita’s *Siddhāntaleśasaṁgraha*):
- **Statement I is correct**: 
  - *Bhāmatī*: The *jīva* is the locus (*āśraya*) of ignorance: *jīvāśrayā viṣayīkṛta-brahma-viṣayā avidyā*. To the objection of mutual dependence (*anyonyāśraya*: jīva depends on avidyā, avidyā depends on jīva), Vācaspati replies that the cycle is beginningless (*bījāṅkuravat anādi*).
  - *Vivaraṇa*: Brahman is pure non-dual consciousness; there is no jīva prior to avidyā. Hence, Brahman alone is both the substrate (*āśraya*) and the object (*viṣaya*) of avidyā (*āśrayatva-viṣayatva-bhāgī hy eka eva cetanaḥ*).
- **Statement II is correct**: 
  - *Bhāmatī* defends **Avacchedavāda**: Just as space (*ākāśa*) appears partitioned into pot-space (*ghaṭākāśa*) by the limiting adjunct of the pot, so the partless Brahman appears limited into *jīvas* by the internal organ (*antaḥkaraṇa*).
  - *Vivaraṇa* defends **Pratibimbavāda**: Brahman is like the original face (*bimba*), and the jīvas are real reflections (*pratibimba*) cast upon the mirror of *avidyā* / *antaḥkaraṇa*. (A variant, *Ābhāsavāda* by Sureśvara, treats the reflection as illusory).
- **Statement III is correct**: 
  - *Bhāmatī*: Verbal testimony (*śabda*) can only produce mediate knowledge (*parokṣa-jñāna*). Direct experience (*aparokṣa*) requires a sensory faculty; hence, the mind (*manas*), concentrated through *nididhyāsana*, is the true *karaṇa* of liberation.
  - *Vivaraṇa*: Words can directly produce immediate perception when the object is already present and self-luminous, as in the famous parable: "You are the tenth man" (*daśamas tvam asi*). The Upanisadic *Mahāvākya* (*Tat Tvam Asi*) directly produces immediate intuition (*aparokṣa-jñāna*).
- **Statement IV is INCORRECT**: Statement IV reverses the positions! The **Bhāmatī** school is famous for admitting a **multiplicity of avidyās** (*nānāvidyā-vāda* / *prati-jīvaṁ bhinnā 'vidyā*) to prevent the flaw that when one person is liberated, all would be liberated. Conversely, the **Vivaraṇa** school champions the doctrine of a **single primal ignorance** (**Mūlāvidyā** / *ekāvidyā-vāda*) accompanied by modal states of ignorance (*tūlāvidyā*).

#### Distractor Analysis:
- Since Statement IV inverts the cardinal positions on *ekāvidyā* vs. *nānāvidyā*, any option containing IV (B, C, and D) is definitively eliminated.

### Question 3 (Format A: Conceptual MCQ - Rāmānuja's Saptavidhā Anupapatti against Avidyā)

In the *Śrībhāṣya* (on *Brahmasūtra* 1.1.1, the *Mahāsiddhānta* section), Rāmānuja mounts his celebrated dialectical assault against the Advaitin doctrine of *Māyā* / *Avidyā*, known as the **Saptavidhā Anupapatti** (Seven Great Untenabilities).

Consider the first two objections:
1. **Āśrayānupapatti** (Untenability of Locus)
2. **Tirodhānānupapatti** (Untenability of Concealment)

Which of the following formulations precisely presents Rāmānuja's dialectical dilemma under **Tirodhānānupapatti**?

A) If Avidyā conceals Brahman, does it conceal Brahman's body or His ornaments? Since Brahman is formless, it cannot conceal anything.  
B) If Avidyā conceals Brahman, this concealment (*tirodhāna*) must mean either the destruction of the luminous nature of Brahman (*prakāśa-nāśa*) or the obstruction of the production of light (*prakāśotpāda-pratibandha*). But since Brahman's nature is eternally unoriginated and self-luminous pure consciousness (*svaprakāśa-svarūpa*), to say that His luminosity is concealed means that His very nature is destroyed, which is an absurdity.  
C) Because concealment can only occur through physical walls, and since Avidyā has no physical bricks, it cannot conceal Brahman.  
D) Because Brahman is concealed only from the perspective of animals, but since humans possess reason, concealment is an epistemic impossibility.

---

#### Correct Answer:
**B) If Avidyā conceals Brahman, this concealment (tirodhāna) must mean either the destruction of the luminous nature of Brahman (prakāśa-nāśa) or the obstruction of the production of light (prakāśotpāda-pratibandha). But since Brahman's nature is eternally unoriginated and self-luminous pure consciousness (svaprakāśa-svarūpa), to say that His luminosity is concealed means that His very nature is destroyed, which is an absurdity.**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Primary Source:
In Rāmānuja’s *Śrībhāṣya* (1.1.1, *Mahāsiddhānta*):
- Rāmānuja formulates the second *anupapatti*—*Tirodhānānupapatti*:
  $$\text{"prakāśa-tirodhānaṁ nāma prakāśa-nāśa eva |}$$
  $$\text{prakāśasya svarūpatve svarūpa-nāśa eva syāt ||"}$$
- The dilemma is devastating:
  1. The Advaitin claims that cosmic *Avidyā* conceals the true nature of Brahman (*Brahma-svarūpāvaraṇa*), causing the world-appearance.
  2. Rāmānuja asks: What is the meaning of "concealment of light" (*prakāśa-tirodhāna*)?
  3. In ordinary life, if light is produced by a lamp, concealment means obstructing the generation of light or obstructing the visual rays.
  4. But in Advaita, Brahman does not *have* consciousness or produce light; Brahman *is* pure consciousness itself (*prakāśa eva svarūpam*).
  5. If the self-luminosity of Brahman is obstructed or obscured by *Avidyā*, that means the light does not shine. But if the light does not shine, then since the light is Brahman’s very essence, Brahman ceases to exist!
  6. To say that eternal, immutable, self-shining consciousness is "covered" while remaining existent is a contradiction in terms. Therefore, *tirodhāna* (concealment) of a purely self-luminous monad by an external principle of darkness is an ontological impossibility.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **Option A is incorrect**: In Advaita, Brahman is Nirguna; Rāmānuja does not argue from physical "ornaments", but from the foundational Advaitic definition of Brahman as *Prakāśa-svarūpa*.
- **Options C and D are incorrect**: These are frivolous trivializations that completely miss Rāmānuja’s technical metaphysical dilemma on *prakāśa-nāśa*.

### Question 4 (Format B: Assertion-Reason - Rāmānuja's Aprthaksiddhi Sambandha)

Given below are two statements: one is labelled as **Assertion (A)** and the other is labelled as **Reason (R)**:

**Assertion (A)**: In Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta, the metaphysical relation between the Supreme Divine (*Īśvara*) and the twin orders of finite reality (*Cit* and *Acit*) is defined as **Apṛthaksiddhi** (inseparable organic relation), conceptualized as the Body-Soul relation (**Śarīra-Śarīrī-bhāva**).  
**Reason (R)**: Because Rāmānuja defines a body (*śarīra*) as any substance which a conscious soul completely controls (*niyāmya*), completely supports (*dhārya*), and utilizes entirely for its own purpose (*śeṣa*); and since individual souls and physical matter have no independent ontological subsistence or capacity to function outside their total dependence on Īśvara, they constitute the eternal body of Īśvara without compromising His absolute divine perfection.

In light of the above statements, choose the correct option:
A) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).  
B) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A).  
C) (A) is true, but (R) is false.  
D) (A) is false, but (R) is true.

---

#### Correct Answer:
**A) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Primary Source:
In Rāmānuja’s *Śrībhāṣya* (on *Brahmasūtra* 2.1.9) and *Vedārthasaṁgraha*:
- Rāmānuja rejects both the Advaitic view (that the world is an unreal appearance, *vivarta*) and the Dvaita view (that souls/matter are completely disconnected, independent substances externally related to God).
- To preserve the absolute non-duality of the organic whole (*Viśiṣṭa-advaita*—non-duality of the qualified whole) while maintaining the reality of the distinct parts, Rāmānuja introduces **Apṛthaksiddhi** (inseparable relation).
- How is this relation formulated? As **Śarīra-Śarīrī-bhāva** (the relation of the body to the embodied soul).
- Rāmānuja supplies the rigorous philosophical definition of *śarīra* (*Śrībhāṣya* 2.1.9):
  $$\text{"yasya cetanasya yad dravyaṁ sarvātmanā svārthe niyantum dhārayituṁ ca śakyam,}$$
  $$\text{tac-cheṣatai-ka-svarūpaṁ ca tat tasya śarīram iti śarīra-lakṣaṇam."}$$
  ("Any substance which a conscious entity can completely control and support for its own purposes, and whose sole essential nature is to be an auxiliary/subservient part [*śeṣa*] to that entity, is the body of that conscious entity.")
- Since all conscious souls (*cit*) and all material nature (*acit*) are supported (*dhārya*), controlled (*niyāmya*), and exist solely to serve the glory (*śeṣin*) of the Supreme Lord (*Parabrahman* / Nārāyaṇa), the cosmos constitutes His living body. Hence, (R) provides the exact, profound theological-metaphysical demonstration of (A).

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **Option B is incorrect**: (R) directly explains the rationale and systematic mechanics of (A).
- **Options C and D are incorrect**: Both statements represent the classical, authentic doctrine of Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta.

### Question 5 (Format A: Conceptual MCQ - Semantic Decryption of "Tat Tvam Asi": Jahadajahallakṣaṇā)

In Advaita hermeneutics, as formulated by Śaṅkara in *Upadeśasāhasrī* (Chapter 18) and Sadānanda in *Vedāntasāra*, the Upanisadic *Mahāvākya* from the *Chāndogya Upaniṣad* (6.8.7):
$$\text{"Tat Tvam Asi"} \quad (\text{"That Thou Art"})$$
is interpreted using the linguistic principle of **Jahad-ajahal-lakṣaṇā** (or *Bhāgatyāga-lakṣaṇā*).

Why does Advaita strictly insist upon *Jahad-ajahal-lakṣaṇā*, rejecting both *Jahallakṣaṇā* (exclusive implication) and *Ajahallakṣaṇā* (inclusive implication)?

A) Because *Tat* and *Tvam* are identical words with identical primary meanings, requiring no hermeneutical interpretation.  
B) Because the primary literal meanings (*vācyārtha*) of *Tat* (Omniscient, transcendent Lord endowed with Māyā) and *Tvam* (finite, embodied jīva subject to ignorance and suffering) are mutually contradictory; therefore, one must abandon the contradictory part of the primary meaning (omniscience vs. limited knowledge) while retaining the non-contradictory common substrate (pure unconditioned Consciousness / *caitanya*).  
C) Because the Vedas are human poetry, and poetry always uses mixed figures of speech.  
D) Because *Tat Tvam Asi* is not an identity statement, but a metaphorical praise command telling the seeker to worship God from a distance.

---

#### Correct Answer:
**B) Because the primary literal meanings (vācyārtha) of Tat (Omniscient, transcendent Lord endowed with Māyā) and Tvam (finite, embodied jīva subject to ignorance and suffering) are mutually contradictory; therefore, one must abandon the contradictory part of the primary meaning (omniscience vs. limited knowledge) while retaining the non-contradictory common substrate (pure unconditioned Consciousness / caitanya).**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Primary Source:
In Sadānanda’s *Vedāntasāra* (sections 148–162) and Dharmarāja Adhvarin’s *Vedāntaparibhāṣā* (Chapter 4):
- In the proposition *Tat Tvam Asi*:
  1. The literal meaning (*vācyārtha*) of **Tat** ("That") is Īśvara: Consciousness qualified by the cosmic adjunct of *Māyā*, characterized by omniscience, omnipotence, and transcendence.
  2. The literal meaning (*vācyārtha*) of **Tvam** ("Thou") is Jīva: Consciousness qualified by the individual adjunct of the *antaḥkaraṇa*, characterized by parviscience, embodiment, agency, and suffering.
  3. A direct literal equation (*sāmānādhikaraṇya*) between an omniscient God and a finite, suffering human being is a logical contradiction, like asserting "fire is cold".
  4. **Why not Jahallakṣaṇā (abandoning the whole meaning)?** In *gaṅgāyāṁ ghoṣaḥ* ("the hamlet on the Ganges"), the river is totally abandoned for the bank. But in *Tat Tvam Asi*, if consciousness is completely abandoned, nothing remains to be equated!
  5. **Why not Ajahallakṣaṇā (retaining the whole meaning)?** In *śoṇo dhāvati* ("the red runs"), the color is retained while the horse is added. But if we retain the qualifiers of *Tat* (omniscience) and *Tvam* (parviscience), the contradiction persists.
  6. **Solution: Jahad-ajahal-lakṣaṇā (Bhāgatyāga-lakṣaṇā)**: Just as in the recognition judgment *"This is that Devadatta"* (*so 'yaṁ devadattaḥ*), one discards the contradictory spatial and temporal qualifications (past time in Varanasi vs. present time in Delhi) and grasps the identical person, so in *Tat Tvam Asi*, one discards the contradictory limiting adjuncts (*upādhis*—omniscience vs. finitude) and directly apprehends the single, undivided, pure substrate consciousness (*akhandaikarasa-caitanya*).

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **Option A is incorrect**: The literal meanings are radically opposed; hence linguistic analysis is indispensable.
- **Option C is incorrect**: Advaita Vedānta regards the Upaniṣads as eternal, authorless divine revelation (*apauruṣeya*), not human poetic caprice.
- **Option D is incorrect**: This is the dualist/theistic reinterpretation advanced by Madhvācārya, who famously reads the passage as *Atat tvam asi* ("Thou art *not* That") or as a subordinate relation; it is the exact antithesis of Advaita.

### Question 6 (Format C: Multi-Statement MSQ - Madhvācārya's Dvaita: Pañcabheda and Viseṣa)

In the *Tattvaviveka* and *Anuvyākhyāna*, Madhvācārya establishes the philosophy of **Dvaita (Bhedavāda)**, asserting that difference (*bheda*) is the foundational, irreducible feature of reality.

Evaluate the following statements regarding Madhva’s metaphysical taxonomy:

I. The **Pañcabheda** (Five Great Differences) are absolute, eternal, and real: difference between God and Jīva, between God and Matter (*Jaḍa*), between Jīva and Matter, between individual Jīvas, and between individual material objects.  
II. Madhva classifies reality into two ultimate ontological orders: **Svatantra** (Independent), which is Lord Viṣṇu alone; and **Paratantra** (Dependent), which encompasses all finite souls and insentient nature, whose existence, knowledge, and activity are eternally subordinate to Viṣṇu.  
III. To explain how an entity can be non-different from its qualities while still permitting conceptual distinctions (e.g., how Viṣṇu is non-different from His bliss, yet we speak of "Viṣṇu's bliss"), Madhva introduces the unique ontological category of **Viśeṣa** (*abhinna-viśeṣa*).  
IV. Madhva rejects the doctrine of *Tāratamya* (spiritual gradation among souls), maintaining that all liberated souls experience identical, uniform bliss in Vaikuṇṭha.

Which of the statements given above are strictly **CORRECT**?

A) I, II, and III only  
B) I and IV only  
C) II, III, and IV only  
D) I, II, III, and IV

---

#### Correct Answer:
**A) I, II, and III only**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Primary Source:
In Madhvācārya’s *Tattvaviveka* (verses 1–3) and Jayatīrtha’s *Nyāyasudhā*:
- **Statement I is correct**: *Tattvaviveka* 1 opens with:
  $$\text{"jīveśvarabhidā caiva jaḍeśvarabhidā tathā |}$$
  $$\text{jīva-bhedo mithaś caiva jaḍa-jīva-bhidā tathā |}$$
  $$\text{mithaś ca jaḍa-bhedo 'yam prapañco bheda-pañcakaḥ ||"}$$
  These five differences constitute the eternal, mind-independent structure of the cosmos.
- **Statement II is correct**: Madhva bifurcates reality into *Svatantra* (the independently real: Lord Nārāyaṇa/Viṣṇu alone) and *Paratantra* (the dependently real: *Cit* and *Acit*). The dependent entities are completely real, but they derive their being (*sattā*), cognition (*pramiti*), and activity (*pravṛtti*) exclusively from the will of the Independent Sovereign.
- **Statement III is correct**: How can we say "the cloth's whiteness" or "Brahman's knowledge" if substance and attribute are identical? The Naiyāyika posits *samavāya* (a distinct relation), which leads to regress. Madhva posits **Viśeṣa**: the inherent capacity in an undivided entity to function as if there were a distinction of relata, without introducing an actual ontological cleavage (*bhedābhāve 'pi bheda-kārya-kāri*).
- **Statement IV is INCORRECT**: Statement IV directly contradicts one of Madhva’s most famous and radical doctrines: **Svarūpa-tāratamya** (the innate hierarchy of souls). Madhva categorizes souls into three eternal classes: *Mukti-yogya* (destined for liberation), *Nitya-saṁsārin* (eternally revolving in transmigration), and *Tamo-yogya* (destined for eternal damnation/Andhatamas). Even among the liberated souls in Vaikuṇṭha, there is an eternal hierarchy (*tāratamya*) of bliss based on their innate spiritual capacity (*svarūpa-ānanda*); the bliss of Brahmā is greater than that of Indra, which is greater than that of ordinary liberated souls.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- Since Statement IV asserts that Madhva rejects *Tāratamya* and accepts uniform bliss (which is the exact opposite of Madhva's doctrine), any option containing IV (B, C, and D) is invalid.

### Question 7 (Format A: Conceptual MCQ - Kashmir Śaivism: Pratyabhijñā and Svātantryavāda)

In the Pratyabhijñā system of Kashmir Śaivism (as codified in Utpaladeva’s *Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā* and Abhinavagupta’s *Tantrāloka* and *Īśvarapratyabhijñāvimarśinī*), the ultimate reality is designated as **Paramasiva**.

How does the Pratyabhijñā doctrine of **Svātantryavāda** (and the dynamic polarity of *Prakāśa* and *Vimarśa*) strictly distinguish itself from Śaṅkara’s Advaita Vedānta?

A) Kashmir Śaivism holds that the universe is made of physical atoms set in motion by mechanical karma, whereas Advaita holds that atoms are non-existent.  
B) While Śaṅkara's Advaita defines ultimate reality as static, featureless consciousness (*Śuddha-Prakāśa* / *Nirviśeṣa-Caitanya*) and relegates dynamic creativity and world-manifestation to an inexplicable principle of illusion (*Māyā* / *Anirvacanīya*), Kashmir Śaivism asserts that consciousness is intrinsically dynamic: it is both self-luminous light (*Prakāśa*) and sovereign self-reflective active consciousness (*Vimarśa* / *Svātantrya*); the world is not an illusion, but the real, joyous self-expansion (*sphurattā* / *unmeṣa*) of Paramasiva appearing on the mirror of His own divine being.  
C) Kashmir Śaivism accepts only the first 24 Sāṁkhya tattvas, whereas Advaita accepts 36 tattvas.  
D) Kashmir Śaivism denies that liberation can ever be attained while living in a physical body (*jīvanmukti*).

---

#### Correct Answer:
**B) While Śaṅkara's Advaita defines ultimate reality as static, featureless consciousness (Śuddha-Prakāśa / Nirviśeṣa-Caitanya) and relegates dynamic creativity and world-manifestation to an inexplicable principle of illusion (Māyā / Anirvacanīya), Kashmir Śaivism asserts that consciousness is intrinsically dynamic: it is both self-luminous light (Prakāśa) and sovereign self-reflective active consciousness (Vimarśa / Svātantrya); the world is not an illusion, but the real, joyous self-expansion (sphurattā / unmeṣa) of Paramasiva appearing on the mirror of His own divine being.**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Primary Source:
In Utpaladeva’s *Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā* (1.5.11–14) and Abhinavagupta’s *Vimarśinī*:
- Utpaladeva critiques the Advaitin conception of Brahman as mere static illumination:
  $$\text{"svāminas ca ātma-saṁsthasya viśvasyātmatayā nayan |}$$
  $$\text{prakāśaḥ syāt tad-arthaṁ tu vimarśaḥ syān na kevalaḥ ||"}$$
  If consciousness were mere *Prakāśa* (bare luminosity without self-reflection), it would be insentient and inert, like a crystal or a mirror reflecting objects passively (*jaḍaprāyo vimarśa-vihīnaḥ prakāśaḥ*).
- What makes consciousness sentient and divine is **Vimarśa**: the sovereign, autonomous power of self-awareness (*ahaṁ-parāmarśa*), creative free will (*svātantrya*), and pulsating energy (*spanda* / *sphurattā*).
- Under **Svātantryavāda** (*Ābhāsavāda*):
  1. The universe is not an illusory projection (*vivarta*) born of cosmic defect or ignorance (*avidyā*).
  2. The universe is the free, glorious self-manifestation (*unmeṣa*) of Paramasiva. Just as a yogi or a king manifests cities within his imagination through his mere will, Paramasiva projects the 36 tattvas of the cosmos upon Himself as the screen (*bhitti*), through His own inherent sovereignty (*svātantrya-śakti*).
  3. The world is entirely real and divine because it is the embodiment of Śiva Himself (*sarvaṁ śivamayam*).

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **Option A is incorrect**: Kashmir Śaivism is an absolute idealist/monist philosophy (*Abheda-vāda*); it vigorously rejects atomistic materialism.
- **Option C is incorrect**: Sāṁkhya has 25 tattvas, Advaita works within the Upanisadic schema, while Kashmir Śaivism is famous for expanding reality into **36 tattvas** (introducing the 5 Pure Tattvas: Śiva, Śakti, Sadāśiva, Īśvara, Śuddhavidyā).
- **Option D is incorrect**: Kashmir Śaivism places immense emphasis on *Jīvanmukti* through spontaneous recognition (*Pratyabhijñā*) of one’s identity with Paramasiva.

### Question 8 (Format B: Assertion-Reason - Advaitic Jīvanmukti and the Destruction of Karmas)

Given below are two statements: one is labelled as **Assertion (A)** and the other is labelled as **Reason (R)**:

**Assertion (A)**: In Advaita Vedānta, upon the dawn of liberating knowledge (*Brahmasākṣātkāra*), the enlightened sage (*Jīvanmukta*) continues to live in the physical body until the exhaustion of his **Prārabdha-karma**, even though his **Sañcita-karma** (accumulated past karmas) and **Āgāmī-karma** (future karmas) are completely incinerated.  
**Reason (R)**: Because *Prārabdha-karma* has already commenced its operational momentum (*pravṛtta-phala*) to generate the present physical embodiment, sensory apparatus, and lifespan, it must run its course to natural termination, like an arrow already released from the archer’s bow (*mukteṣuvat*) or the continuing rotation of a potter’s wheel (*cakrabhramavat*) after the potter has ceased to spin it.

In light of the above statements, choose the correct option:
A) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).  
B) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A).  
C) (A) is true, but (R) is false.  
D) (A) is false, but (R) is true.

---

#### Correct Answer:
**A) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Primary Source:
In Śaṅkarācārya’s commentary on *Brahmasūtra* (4.1.13–15) and *Chāndogya Upaniṣad* (6.14.2: *tasya tāvad eva ciraṁ yāvan na vimokṣye 'tha sampatsye*):
- Śaṅkara delineates the three-fold classification of Karma:
  1. *Sañcita-karma*: Stored past karmas that have not yet begun to bear fruit. These are completely destroyed like roasted seeds by knowledge (*jñānāgniḥ sarvakarmāṇi bhasmasāt kurute*, Gītā 4.37).
  2. *Āgāmī-karma*: Future actions performed after enlightenment. These do not cling to the sage, like water on a lotus leaf (*padmapatram ivāmbhasā*).
  3. *Prārabdha-karma*: That portion of past karma which has already begun to bear fruit (*pravṛtta-kārya*) by manufacturing the current physical body.
- Śaṅkara explains why *Prārabdha* is not extinguished instantly at the moment of realization:
  Knowledge destroys ignorance and potential future causal chains, but it cannot undo a momentum already actualized in the physical cosmos. 
  Just as an arrow that has been loosed from the bow cannot be recalled in mid-air and will fly until its kinetic energy is spent (*yathā bāṇo muktaḥ vegakṣayāt patati*), or just as a potter's wheel continues to spin for a time by past inertia (*cakra-bhramaṇavat*) even after the potter removes his stick, so the physical body of the sage persists until the momentum of *Prārabdha* is exhausted. When *Prārabdha* terminates, the sage attains unembodied liberation (*Videhamukti*). Hence, (R) provides the definitive classical explanation for (A).

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **Option B is incorrect**: (R) is the exact canonical analogy and logical mechanism that justifies (A).
- **Options C and D are incorrect**: Both statements are direct reproductions of Advaita Vedānta’s classic soteriological doctrine.

### Question 9 (Format A: Conceptual MCQ - Nimbārka's Svābhāvika-Bhedābhedavāda)

In the Vedāntic tradition, **Nimbārkācārya** (in his *Vedāntapārijātasaurabha*) propounds the philosophy of **Svābhāvika-Bhedābhedavāda** (Natural Difference-in-Non-Difference).

How does Nimbārka’s doctrine strictly distinguish itself from the *Aupādhika-Bhedābhedavāda* of **Bhāskarācārya**?

A) Bhāskara holds that difference is beginningless and eternal, whereas Nimbārka holds that difference is a complete illusion.  
B) Bhāskara asserts that non-difference (*abheda*) is natural and real (*svābhāvika*), while difference (*bheda*) is adventitious and caused by extrinsic limiting adjuncts (*aupādhika*), disappearing upon liberation; whereas Nimbārka asserts that BOTH difference (*bheda*) and non-difference (*abheda*) are co-equally natural, real, and eternal (*svābhāvika*), persisting even in the state of final liberation.  
C) Bhāskara worships Śiva, while Nimbārka worships Buddha.  
D) Bhāskara denies the authority of the Upaniṣads, while Nimbārka accepts only the *Pāñcarātra Āgamas*.

---

#### Correct Answer:
**B) Bhāskara asserts that non-difference (abheda) is natural and real (svābhāvika), while difference (bheda) is adventitious and caused by extrinsic limiting adjuncts (aupādhika), disappearing upon liberation; whereas Nimbārka asserts that BOTH difference (bheda) and non-difference (abheda) are co-equally natural, real, and eternal (svābhāvika), persisting even in the state of final liberation.**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Primary Source:
In Bhāskarācārya’s *Brahmasūtrabhāṣya* and Nimbārkācārya’s *Vedāntapārijātasaurabha* (with Śrīnivāsa’s *Vedāntakaustubha*):
- **Bhāskara’s Aupādhika-Bhedābheda**:
  - Non-difference (*abheda*) is the innate, essential, and eternal nature of Brahman (*svābhāvika*).
  - Difference (*bheda*) is temporary and adventitious (*aupādhika*), generated by the physical and mental adjuncts (*upādhis*). When the adjuncts dissolve in liberation (*mukti*), the difference disappears completely, and the soul merges into non-differentiated identity with Brahman.
- **Nimbārka’s Svābhāvika-Bhedābheda**:
  - Nimbārka rejects Bhāskara's asymmetry. He argues that if difference were merely *aupādhika* (due to adjuncts), it would either be unreal (leading back to Śaṅkara's *Māyā*) or an unexplained defect corrupting Brahman.
  - Therefore, Nimbārka maintains that **both difference and non-difference are equally natural and eternal (svābhāvika)**.
  - The relation is analogized to the sun and its rays (*sūrya-kiraṇa*) or the sea and its waves (*taraṅga*). The rays are non-different from the sun because they cannot exist independently of it; yet they are different from the sun because an individual ray is not the entire solar orb.
  - The individual soul (*jīva*) and matter (*acit*) are different from Brahman because they have distinct dependent natures; yet they are non-different because their very existence and functioning are inseparably sustained by Brahman. This dual status of *bheda* and *abheda* endures even in *mokṣa*.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- **Option A is incorrect**: Bhāskara never treats difference as eternal; he explicitly treats it as temporary (*aupādhika*).
- **Options C and D are incorrect**: Both Bhāskara and Nimbārka are orthodox Vedāntins who accept the Upaniṣads and the *Brahmasūtra*; neither worships the Buddha or rejects Vedāntic authority.

### Question 10 (Format D: Comparative Matching - The Five Vedāntic Schools and Their Causal Doctrines)

Match the Vedāntic traditions in **Column I** with their primary Acharyas in **Column II** and their definitive ontological/causal doctrines in **Column III**:

| Column I (Tradition) | Column II (Foundational Ācārya) | Column III (Definitive Metaphysical Doctrine) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **P. Kevalādvaita** | 1. Rāmānujācārya | i. *Vivartavāda*: World is an apparent manifestation of Brahman via inexplicable *Māyā*. |
| **Q. Viśiṣṭādvaita** | 2. Śaṅkarācārya | ii. *Brahmapariṇāmavāda* qualified by *Apṛthaksiddhi-sambandha* (*Śarīra-Śarīrī-bhāva*). |
| **R. Dvaita** | 3. Madhvācārya | iii. *Asatkāryavāda-Paratantravāda*: Eternal *Pañcabheda* sustained by the Independent Will of Viṣṇu. |
| **S. Śuddhādvaita** | 4. Vallabhācārya | iv. *Avi-kṛta-Brahmapariṇāmavāda*: Brahman manifests as the cosmos without undergoing any deformation or dependence on Māyā. |

Choose the correct matching code:
A) P-2-i, Q-1-ii, R-3-iii, S-4-iv  
B) P-1-i, Q-2-ii, R-4-iii, S-3-iv  
C) P-2-iv, Q-1-ii, R-3-i, S-4-iii  
D) P-3-iii, Q-4-iv, R-1-ii, S-2-i  

---

#### Correct Answer:
**A) P-2-i, Q-1-ii, R-3-iii, S-4-iv**

#### Philosophical Grounding & Primary Source:
1. **Kevalādvaita (Śaṅkarācārya)**: Upholds *Vivartavāda*—the world is an apparent, non-transformational appearance (*atasattva-anyathā-bhāva*) superimposed upon Brahman through *Māyā* -> **P-2-i**.
2. **Viśiṣṭādvaita (Rāmānujācārya)**: Upholds a qualified *Brahmapariṇāmavāda* through the *Śarīra-Śarīrī-bhāva* (the subtle body of Cit and Acit expands into the gross body of the universe while Brahman's essential nature remains immutable) -> **Q-1-ii**.
3. **Dvaita (Madhvācārya)**: Upholds absolute realism and *Pañcabheda* under the sovereignty of *Svatantra* Viṣṇu -> **R-3-iii**.
4. **Śuddhādvaita (Vallabhācārya)**: In *Anubhāṣya*, Vallabha propounds **Avikṛta-Pariṇāmavāda** ("unmodified transformation"): Brahman manifests as the universe by His sheer will, concealing His qualities of consciousness and bliss (*tirobhāva*) without undergoing any deformation (*vikāra*) and without needing the assistance of an illusory principle called *Māyā* ("Śuddha" = pure non-dualism untainted by Māyā) -> **S-4-iv**.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- Any variation from **A (P-2-i, Q-1-ii, R-3-iii, S-4-iv)** confuses the defining doctrinal taxonomy of the Vedānta traditions. For instance, swapping Vallabha's *Śuddhādvaita* with Śaṅkara's *Kevalādvaita* ignores the critical distinction between *Vivarta* and *Avikṛta-Pariṇāma*.
