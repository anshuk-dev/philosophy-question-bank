# Comprehensive Philosophy Master Syllabus & Exam Blueprint
## For Assistant Professor Interviews & Exams (IITs, Central Universities, GATE XH-C4, UGC NET Paper 88/03, JNUEE)

### Structure of the Curriculum & Examination Standard
This repository is engineered to provide an authoritative, high-difficulty, conceptually rigorous question bank and mock test series for candidates preparing for faculty positions (Assistant Professor) in Philosophy across Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs) and Central Universities (such as JNU, DU, HCU, BHU).

The questions follow the exact demanding standards of:
1. **GATE Philosophy (XH-C4)**: Emphasis on logical rigor, formal and informal inferences, conceptual tweaking, multi-statement evaluation (MSQ style), and technical precision.
2. **UGC NET Philosophy (Paper II)**: Exhaustive coverage of classical and contemporary systems, assertion-reasoning, textual precision, matching pairs, and chronological/doctrinal lineage.
3. **JNUEE / Research Entrance Standard**: Deep interpretive questions, critical counter-arguments, cross-tradition comparative philosophy (e.g. Navya-Nyaya vs Frege-Russell on sense/reference; Advaita vs Kant on transcendental illusion; Madhyamaka vs Pyrrhonism; Heidegger vs Sartre on ontology).

### Core Domain Breakdown

#### 1. Classical Indian Philosophy
- **Vedic & Upanisadic Foundations**: Rta, Satya, Rna, Yajna, Karma, rebirth; Atman and Brahman in Mandukya (four states: Jagrat, Svapna, Susupti, Turiya), Chandogya, Brhadaranyaka, Katha.
- **Heterodox Systems**:
  - *Carvaka*: Epistemology (rejection of Anumana, Sabda, Upamana; critique of Vyapti), Metaphysics (Dehatmavada, Bhutacaitanyavada), Ethics (Kama as highest purusartha).
  - *Jainism*: Anekantavada, Syadvada (Saptabhangi-naya), Nayavada; Dravya, Guna, Paryaya; Jiva and Ajiva (Pudgala, Dharma, Adharma, Akasa, Kala); Asrava, Bandha, Samvara, Nirjara, Moksa; Triratna.
  - *Buddhism*: Pratityasamutpada (12 Nidanas), Four Noble Truths, Anatmavada, Ksanikavada, Arthakriyakaritva; Sautrantika (Bahyanumeyavada, Svalaksana vs Samanyalaksana), Vaibhasika (Sarvastivada, Bahyapratyaksavada), Yogacara (Vijnanavada, Trisvabhava: Parikalpita, Paratantra, Parinispanna; Alayavijnana), Madhyamaka (Nagarjuna, Sunyata as Pratityasamutpada, Samvrti vs Paramartha satya, Catuskoti).
- **Orthodox Systems (Sad-darsana)**:
  - *Nyaya*: 16 Padarthas; Four Pramanas; Pratyaksa (Nirvikalpaka, Savikalpaka, Laukika/Alaukika sannikarsa - Samanyalaksana, Jnanalaksana, Yogaja); Anumana (Svartha vs Parartha, Panca-avayava; Purvavat, Sesavat, Samanyatodrsta; Kevalanvayi, Kevalavyatireki, Anvayavyatireki); Vyapti and Paramarsa; Five Hetvabhasas (Savyabhicara, Viruddha, Satpratipaksa, Asiddha, Badhita); Upamana; Sabda (Saktigraha, Tatparya, Akanksa, Yogyata, Sannidhi); Asatkaryavada (Arambhavada); Isvara proofs; Apavarga.
  - *Navya-Nyaya*: Gangesa, Raghunatha Siromani; Avacchedaka, Pratiyogita, Svarupasambandha; Vyapti definitions (Vyaptipancaka and Siddhantalaksana).
  - *Vaisesika*: 7 Padarthas (Dravya, Guna, Karma, Samanya, Visesa, Samavaya, Abhava: Pragabhava, Pradhvamsabhava, Atyantabhava, Anyonyabhava); Paramanuvada; Pilupaka vs Pitharapaka.
  - *Samkhya*: Satkaryavada (Parinamavada); Prakrti and its Gunas (Sattva, Rajas, Tamas); Purusa (plurality of selves, witness consciousness); 25 Tattvas evolution scheme; Kaivalya (Vivekajnana).
  - *Yoga*: Patanjali; Cittavrtti and Cittabhumi; Astanga Yoga; Samprasada, Samprajnata and Asamprajnata Samadhi; Klesas; Isvarapranidhana.
  - *Purva Mimamsa*: Jaimini, Kumarila Bhatta, Prabhakara; Pramanas (Bhatta: 6 pramanas including Arthapatti and Anupalabdhi; Prabhakara: 5 pramanas rejecting Anupalabdhi); Epistemology of validity (Svatah-pramanyavada vs Paratah-pramanyavada); Perception theories (Triputipratyaksavada vs Jnatatavada); Khyativada (Akhyati of Prabhakara vs Viparitakhyati of Bhatta); Dharma, Vidhi, Arthavada, Apurva.
- **Vedanta Traditions**:
  - *Advaita Vedanta*: Sankara, Gaudapada (Ajativada); Nirguna Brahman; Maya and Avidya (Avarana and Viksepa saktis); Vivartavada; Adhyasa; Three orders of reality (Pratibhasika, Vyavaharika, Paramarthika); Anirvacaniya-khyati; Mahavakyas; Jivanmukti vs Videhamukti. Post-Sankara schools (Bhamati of Vacaspati Misra vs Vivarana of Prakasatman).
  - *Visistadvaita*: Ramanuja; Saguna Brahman (Parabrahman with infinite auspicious qualities); Aprthaksiddhi sambandha; Cit, Acit, Isvara (Tattvatraya); Brahmaparinamavada; Bhakti and Prapatti; Sat-khyati; Rejection of Maya (Saptavidha Anupapatti).
  - *Dvaita*: Madhva; Bhedavada (Pancabheda); Taratamya; Svarupa-bheda; Svatantra vs Paratantra; Abhinnavisesa; Bhakti.
  - *Other Vedanta*: Nimbarka (Dvaitadvaita), Vallabha (Suddhadvaita, Pustimarga), Baladeva/Caitanya (Acintyabhedabheda).
  - *Kashmir Saivism*: Pratyabhijna system (Abhinavagupta, Utpaladeva); Spanda; Svatantryavada; 36 Tattvas; Paramasiva.

#### 2. Western Philosophy: Ancient, Medieval & Modern
- **Ancient Greek**:
  - *Presocratics*: Thales, Anaximander, Anaximenes, Heraclitus (Logos, Unity of Opposites), Parmenides (Being, Way of Truth vs Way of Seeming), Zeno's paradoxes, Empedocles, Anaxagoras (Nous), Democritus (Atomism).
  - *Socrates & Sophists*: Protagoras ("Man is the measure"), Gorgias; Socratic Irony, Elenchus, "Virtue is Knowledge", Akrasia.
  - *Plato*: Theory of Forms (Eidos), Third Man Argument, Divided Line, Allegory of the Cave, Tripartite Soul, Justice in *Republic*, Recollection (Anamnesis) in *Meno* and *Phaedo*, Knowledge in *Theaetetus*, Critique of Forms in *Parmenides*.
  - *Aristotle*: Critique of Platonic Forms; Hylomorphism (Form and Matter); Four Causes (Material, Formal, Efficient, Final); Actuality and Potentiality (Energeia/Dynamis); Categories and Ousia (Primary vs Secondary Substance); Nicomachean Ethics (Eudaimonia, Ergon argument, Doctrine of the Mean, Phronesis, Akrasia); Unmoved Mover.
  - *Hellenistic*: Stoic Logic and Physics (Logos, Apatheia), Epicurean Atomism and Hedonism (Ataraxia, Katastematic vs Kinetic pleasure), Pyrrhonism (Sextus Empiricus, Tropes, Epoché, Ataraxia).
- **Medieval Philosophy**:
  - St. Augustine: Illumination theory, Problem of Evil (*privatio boni*), Free Will, Time as subjective distension.
  - St. Anselm: Ontological Argument (*Proslogion* II & III), Gaunilo's reply ("Lost Island") and Anselm's rejoinder.
  - St. Thomas Aquinas: Faith and Reason, Quinque Viae (Five Ways), Doctrine of Analogy (*analogia entis*), Essence and Existence (*esse*), Natural Law.
  - Universals: Extreme Realism (Plato, William of Champeaux), Moderate Realism (Aristotle, Aquinas), Conceptualism (Abelard), Nominalism (Roscelin, William of Ockham - Ockham's Razor).
- **Modern Philosophy: Rationalism vs Empiricism**:
  - *Descartes*: Method of Doubt, *Cogito Ergo Sum*, Clear and Distinct Ideas, Ontological & Trademark arguments for God, Mind-Body Dualism (Interactionism, Pineal gland), Cartesian Circle.
  - *Spinoza*: Monism (*Deus sive Natura*), Infinite Attributes (Thought and Extension), Infinite and Finite Modes, Psycho-physical Parallelism, Determinism and *Natura naturans* vs *Natura naturata*, *Conatus*, *Amor intellectualis Dei*.
  - *Leibniz*: Monadology, Pre-established Harmony, Truths of Reason vs Truths of Fact, Principle of Contradiction, Principle of Sufficient Reason, Identity of Indiscernibles, Theodicy (Best of all possible worlds).
  - *Locke*: Rejection of Innate Ideas, *Tabula Rasa*, Sensation and Reflection, Simple and Complex Ideas, Primary vs Secondary Qualities, Substance as "something I know not what", Personal Identity as Psychological Continuity (Memory).
  - *Berkeley*: Subjective Idealism (*Esse est percipi aut percipere*), Critique of Abstract Ideas, Critique of Primary-Secondary quality distinction, Master Argument, God as the eternal perceiver.
  - *Hume*: Impressions and Ideas, Hume's Fork (Relations of Ideas vs Matters of Fact), Critique of Causality (Constant Conjunction, Psychological habit, lack of necessary connection), Bundle Theory of the Self, Problem of Induction, Critique of Miracles, Is-Ought Problem.
- **Critical Philosophy & German Idealism**:
  - *Kant*: Copernican Revolution; Possibility of Synthetic *a priori* propositions; Transcendental Aesthetic (Space and Time as *a priori* forms of intuition); Transcendental Analytic (Categories of the Understanding, Transcendental Deduction of the Categories, Schematism); Transcendental Dialectic (Paralogisms of Pure Reason, Antinomies of Pure Reason, Ideal of Pure Reason, Noumena vs Phenomena); Practical Reason (Autonomy, Categorical Imperative: Formula of Universal Law, Formula of Humanity, Kingdom of Ends, Postulates of Practical Reason); Judgment (Purposiveness, Sublime, Teleology).
  - *German Idealism*: Fichte (Transcendental Ego and Non-Ego), Schelling (Identity of Subject and Object), Hegel (Absolute Idealism, Dialectical Movement: Thesis-Antithesis-Synthesis, Master-Slave Dialectic, Alienation, *Geist*, Philosophy of Right).

#### 3. Contemporary Analytic Philosophy, Logic, and Philosophy of Language
- **Foundational Analytic Philosophy**:
  - *Frege*: Sense and Reference (*Sinn und Bedeutung*), Context Principle, Concept and Object, Anti-psychologism, Foundations of Arithmetic.
  - *Russell*: Theory of Definite Descriptions ("On Denoting", logical form vs grammatical form), Logical Atomism, Theory of Types, Knowledge by Acquaintance and Knowledge by Description.
  - *Early Wittgenstein*: *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus* (Picture Theory of Meaning, Logical Form, Truth-tables and Tautologies, Sayable vs Showable, Solipsism, Nonsense of Metaphysics).
  - *Logical Positivism*: Vienna Circle (Schlick, Carnap, Neurath), A.J. Ayer (*Language, Truth and Logic*); Verification Principle (strong vs weak verification), Protocol sentences, Emotive theory of ethics, Elimination of metaphysics, Carnap's Internal vs External questions.
- **Ordinary Language & Late Analytic Philosophy**:
  - *Late Wittgenstein*: *Philosophical Investigations* (Meaning as use, Language games, Forms of life, Family resemblance, Critique of ostensive definition, Private Language Argument, Rule-Following paradox).
  - *G.E. Moore*: Defense of Common Sense, Proof of an External World, Refutation of Idealism.
  - *J.L. Austin*: Speech Act Theory (Constatives vs Performatives, Locutionary, Illocutionary, Perlocutionary acts, Felicity conditions).
  - *P.F. Strawson*: "On Referring" (critique of Russell's descriptions), Descriptive vs Revisionary Metaphysics, Basic particulars.
- **Post-Positivism & Contemporary Epistemology/Metaphysics**:
  - *W.V.O. Quine*: "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" (Critique of Analytic-Synthetic distinction and Reductionism), Holism (Web of Belief), Indeterminacy of Translation, Inscrutability of Reference, Ontological Relativity, Epistemology Naturalized.
  - *Wilfrid Sellars*: Myth of the Given, Space of Reasons.
  - *Donald Davidson*: Truth-conditional semantics (T-sentences), Radical Interpretation, Principle of Charity, Anomalous Monism.
  - *Saul Kripke*: *Naming and Necessity* (Rigid designators, Causal theory of reference, Necessary *a posteriori*, Contingent *a priori*), *Wittgenstein on Rules and Private Language* (Kripkenstein skeptic).
  - *Hilary Putnam*: Semantic Externalism ("Twin Earth", "Meanings just ain't in the head"), Brain in a Vat, Machine Functionalism and Multiple Realizability, Critique of Fact-Value dichotomy.
  - *Epistemology*: Gettier Problem (1963 paper, JTB counter-examples: Smith and Jones, Nogot and Havit), Post-Gettier responses (No False Premises, Defeasibility, Causal Theory of Goldman, Tracking Theory of Nozick), Reliabilism, Virtue Epistemology (Ernest Sosa: Animal vs Reflective knowledge, Linda Zagzebski), Epistemic Contextualism.
- **Formal Logic**:
  - Propositional logic (Syntax, Semantics, Truth tables, Formal proofs, Tautology, Contradiction, Contingency).
  - Predicate logic (Monadic and Polyadic predicates, Quantifiers, Domain of discourse, Identity).
  - Fallacies: Formal (Affirming the consequent, Denying the antecedent, Undistributed middle) and Informal (Ad hominem, Petitio principii, Equivocation, Composition, Division, Straw man, False dichotomy).
  - Modal logic basics (Alethic modalities, Systems K, T, S4, S5, Possible Worlds semantics).

#### 4. Continental Philosophy and Philosophy of Mind
- **Phenomenology & Hermeneutics**:
  - *Franz Brentano*: Intentionality as the mark of the mental.
  - *Edmund Husserl*: *Epoché* (Phenomenological reduction), Eidetic reduction, Transcendental reduction, Intentionality, *Noesis* and *Noema*, Horizons, Intersubjectivity, *Lebenswelt* (Lifeworld).
  - *Maurice Merleau-Ponty*: *Phenomenology of Perception*, Embodied Consciousness (*corps propre*), Primacy of Perception, The Flesh (*la chair*), Chiasm.
  - *Hermeneutics*: Schleiermacher, Dilthey; Hans-Georg Gadamer (*Truth and Method*, Hermeneutic circle, Prejudice/*Vorurteil*, Fusion of Horizons/*Horizontverschmelzung*, Linguisticality of understanding); Paul Ricoeur (Hermeneutics of suspicion vs faith, Narrative identity).
- **Existentialism & Post-Structuralism**:
  - *Søren Kierkegaard*: Three stages of life (Aesthetic, Ethical, Religious), Leap of Faith, Anguish/*Angst*, Subjectivity is Truth, Abraham in *Fear and Trembling*.
  - *Friedrich Nietzsche*: Will to Power (*Wille zur Macht*), Master and Slave Morality, Revaluation of All Values, Death of God, *Übermensch*, Eternal Recurrence, Perspectivism.
  - *Martin Heidegger*: *Being and Time* (*Sein und Zeit*), The Question of Being (*Seinsfrage*), *Dasein*, Being-in-the-world (*In-der-Welt-sein*), Ready-to-hand (*Zuhandenheit*) vs Present-at-hand (*Vorhandenheit*), *Das Man* (The "They"), Authenticity (*Eigentlichkeit*), Being-towards-death (*Sein-zum-Tode*), Care (*Sorge*), The Turn (*Kehre*), Critique of modern technology (Enframing/*Gestell*).
  - *Jean-Paul Sartre*: *Being and Nothingness*, Being-in-itself (*en-soi*) vs Being-for-itself (*pour-soi*), Bad Faith (*mauvaise foi*), Radical Freedom and Responsibility, "Existence precedes Essence", The Look of the Other (*Le Regard*).
  - *Albert Camus*: The Absurd, *The Myth of Sisyphus*, Philosophical Suicide vs Revolt.
  - *Michel Foucault*: *Madness and Civilization*, *The Order of Things* (Episteme), *Discipline and Punish* (Panopticism, Docile bodies), *The History of Sexuality*, Power/Knowledge (*pouvoir-savoir*), Biopolitics, Governmentality.
  - *Jacques Derrida*: Deconstruction, Critique of Logocentrism and Metaphysics of Presence, *Différance*, Trace, *Pharmakon*, "There is no outside-text" (*Il n'y a pas de hors-texte*).
  - *Critical Theory*: Adorno & Horkheimer (*Dialectic of Enlightenment*, Culture Industry); Jürgen Habermas (Theory of Communicative Action, Ideal Speech Situation, Lifeworld vs System colonized).
- **Philosophy of Mind**:
  - Mind-Body Problem: Substance Dualism (Descartes), Property Dualism, Epiphenomenalism.
  - Identity Theories: Type Physicalism (U.T. Place, J.J.C. Smart), Token Physicalism.
  - Behaviorism: Methodological (Watson, Skinner) vs Logical/Philosophical (Gilbert Ryle: Category Mistake, "Ghost in the Machine", Knowing-how vs Knowing-that).
  - Functionalism: Machine State Functionalism (Putnam), Teleological Functionalism, Multiple Realizability argument.
  - Consciousness and Qualia:
    * Thomas Nagel: "What is it like to be a bat?" (Subjective character of experience).
    * Frank Jackson: Knowledge Argument (Mary the colour scientist, Epiphenomenal qualia).
    * David Chalmers: The "Hard Problem" of consciousness vs "Easy problems", Philosophical Zombies, Explanatory Gap (Levine), Dual-aspect theory/Panpsychism.
    * John Searle: Chinese Room thought experiment, Syntax vs Semantics, Biological Naturalism.
    * Daniel Dennett: Multiple Drafts model, Heterophenomenology, Intentional Stance.

#### 5. Ethics, Social & Political Philosophy, and Applied Ethics
- **Normative Ethics**:
  - *Utilitarianism & Consequentialism*: Jeremy Bentham (Hedonistic calculus, Quantitative utilitarianism); J.S. Mill (Qualitative hedonism, Higher vs Lower pleasures, Proof of utility); Act vs Rule Utilitarianism (Smart vs Brandt); Preference Utilitarianism (Peter Singer).
  - *Deontological Ethics*: Immanuel Kant (Good Will, Duty vs Inclination, Categorical Imperative, Hypothetical Imperative, Perfect vs Imperfect duties); W.D. Ross (The Right and the Good, Prima Facie duties vs Actual duty).
  - *Virtue Ethics*: Aristotle (Eudaimonia, Hexis, Virtue as Mean between extremes); Elizabeth Anscombe ("Modern Moral Philosophy"); Philippa Foot (Trolley problem, Natural goodness); Alasdair MacIntyre (*After Virtue*, Practices, Narrative unity of life).
- **Meta-Ethics**:
  - Cognitivism vs Non-Cognitivism.
  - G.E. Moore: *Principia Ethica*, The Naturalistic Fallacy, Open Question Argument, Intuitionism, Non-natural moral properties.
  - Non-Cognitivism: Emotivism (Ayer's "Boo-Hurrah" theory, C.L. Stevenson), Prescriptivism (R.M. Hare: Universal prescriptivism).
  - Moral Realism vs Anti-Realism: J.L. Mackie (Moral Skepticism, Error Theory: Argument from Relativity, Argument from Queerness); Quasi-realism (Simon Blackburn); Moral Relativism (Harman).
- **Applied Ethics**:
  - Bioethics: Abortion (Thomson's violinist analogy, Don Marquis's "future-like-ours", Michael Tooley on personhood), Euthanasia (James Rachels on active vs passive distinction), Genetic enhancement.
  - Environmental Ethics: Anthropocentrism vs Non-anthropocentrism; Peter Singer (Speciesism, Animal Liberation); Tom Regan (The Case for Animal Rights, Subject-of-a-life); Arne Naess (Deep Ecology, Biocentric egalitarianism); Aldo Leopold (Land Ethic).
  - Ethics of Technology & AI: Alignment problem, Moral status of AI, Value lock-in.
- **Social and Political Philosophy**:
  - *Classical Social Contract*: Thomas Hobbes (State of Nature as *bellum omnium contra omnes*, Leviathan, Absolute sovereignty); John Locke (State of Nature governed by Natural Law, Property rights, Consent, Right to resistance); Jean-Jacques Rousseau (State of Nature, Social Contract, General Will vs Will of All, Forced to be free).
  - *Theories of Justice*:
    * John Rawls: *A Theory of Justice*, Original Position, Veil of Ignorance, Maximin rule, Principles of Justice (1. Greatest Equal Liberty, 2a. Fair Equality of Opportunity, 2b. Difference Principle), Lexical priority, Reflective Equilibrium, *Political Liberalism* (Overlapping Consensus, Public Reason).
    * Robert Nozick: *Anarchy, State, and Utopia*, Libertarianism, Entitlement Theory of Justice (Justice in Acquisition, Justice in Transfer, Rectification), Historical vs Patterned principles, Wilt Chamberlain argument, Minimal State.
    * Communitarianism: Michael Sandel (*Liberalism and the Limits of Justice*, Critique of the Unencumbered Self), Michael Walzer (*Spheres of Justice*), Charles Taylor (Atomism critique).
  - *Marxism & Critical Social Theory*: Karl Marx (Historical Materialism, Dialectical Materialism, Theory of Alienation from product, act, species-essence, others; Base and Superstructure, Commodity Fetishism, Surplus Value).
  - *Modern Indian Thinkers*:
    * Swami Vivekananda: Practical Vedanta, Neo-Vedanta, Harmony of religions, Spiritual humanism.
    * Sri Aurobindo: Integral Advaita, Involution and Evolution, Supermind, Gnostic Being, The Life Divine.
    * Mahatma Gandhi: Satyagraha, Ahimsa, Swaraj (moral self-mastery and political independence), Sarvodaya, Trusteeship, Critique of modern civilization in *Hind Swaraj*.
    * B.R. Ambedkar: Critique of Caste system, *Annihilation of Caste*, Navayana Buddhism, Social Democracy ("Liberty, Equality, Fraternity"), Constitutional Morality.
    * Rabindranath Tagore: Humanism (*The Religion of Man*), Critique of aggressive nationalism.
    * M.N. Roy: Radical Humanism / Scientific Humanism.
    * K.C. Bhattacharyya: *Swaraj in Ideas*, *The Subject as Freedom*, Concept of the Absolute (as Truth, as Freedom, as Value).

### Question Specifications: "High Conceptual & Critical Rigor"
Every question generated in this bank MUST strictly satisfy the following criteria:
1. **Target Audience**: Candidates facing interview panels of Senior Professors at IITs and Central Universities.
2. **No Triviality**: Avoid rote-memory recall (e.g. "Who wrote Meditations?"). Focus on subtle doctrinal pivots, counter-arguments, inner tensions, and technical distinctions (e.g., "Why does Ramanuja hold that even the illusion of silver in shell is sat-khyati?", or "Why does Kripke reject Frege-Russell descriptive theory for proper names using modal arguments rather than epistemic arguments?").
3. **Four Question Formats**:
   - Format A: **Rigorous 4-Option Conceptual MCQ** with razor-sharp distractors.
   - Format B: **Assertion (A) and Reason (R)**, where candidates must distinguish whether R is the *correct explanation* of A or merely a true independent statement, or whether either is false.
   - Format C: **Multi-Statement Evaluation (MSQ / GATE style)**: Statements I, II, III, IV given; determine which combination is strictly correct.
   - Format D: **Comparative / Doctrinal Matching**: Subtle philosophical theses mapped against their technical exponents and exact textual counter-theses.
4. **Mandatory Exhaustive Explanation Structure**:
   - **Correct Answer**: State clearly which option is correct.
   - **Philosophical Grounding & Demonstration**: Rigorous primary-source derivation explaining why this option is philosophically and textually true.
   - **Distractor Analysis (Crucial)**: Explicitly break down EACH wrong option and demonstrate why it is false, what fallacy it commits, or which rival school it actually belongs to.
   - **Primary Source / Textual Reference**: Cite the classic foundational treatise (e.g., *Slokavarttika*, *Tattvacintamani*, *Brahmasutrabhasya*, *Critique of Pure Reason* A/B edition, *Philosophical Investigations*, *Word and Object*, etc.).
